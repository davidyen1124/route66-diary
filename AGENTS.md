# Prototype Instructions

Run the local server yourself and open the preview in the browser available to this environment. Do not give the user server-start instructions when you can run it.

Before making substantial visual changes, use the Product Design plugin's `get-context` skill when the visual source is unclear or no longer matches the current goal. When the user gives durable prototype-specific design feedback, preferences, or decisions, record them in `AGENTS.md`.

When implementing from a selected generated mock, treat that image as the source of truth for layout, component anatomy, density, spacing, color, typography, visible content, and hierarchy.

Build app UI in `src/`. Keep `.openai/hosting.json`, `worker/index.js`, `scripts/prepare-sites-build.mjs`, and `tests/sites-worker.test.mjs` intact so the same local prototype can be handed to Sites. Before a Sites handoff, run `npm run build` and `npm run test:sites`; the build must leave `dist/client/index.html`, `dist/server/index.js`, and `dist/.openai/hosting.json`.

## Route 66 diary design decisions

The site is a pixel-faithful homage to the 1985 Apple II *The Oregon Trail* (MECC). Reference captures and measurements are summarized in `design-qa.md`.

- Every page is a stack of 280x192 Apple II hi-res screens drawn to canvas by `src/apple2/`. Screens keep the game's layouts: title/menu screens between blue dividers, the travel screen (scenery band, sky, ground block, message box, "Press RETURN to size up the situation", white status box), landmark pictures with a white caption bar, text screens between dividers, and "What is your choice?" prompts with the checkered cursor.
- Colors come only from the hi-res bit model in `src/apple2/hires.js` (black, white, green, violet, orange, blue plus NTSC fringing). Do not add CSS colors, gradients, or smoothing to the screens. The page surround is flat `#111`.
- Text uses the game's measured proportional font and big title font in `src/apple2/fontData.js`. Glyphs not seen in the captures are drawn in the same 2-pixel stroke style. Line pitch is 9 pixels and body text starts at x=20.
- One screen at a time, never a scrolling page, on desktop and mobile (`src/components/Monitor.jsx`). The current screen fills the window, scaled by whole device pixels when that fills at least 90% of the space (otherwise it fills the space). Its id is kept in the URL hash (for example `#stop-3-1`) so refreshes and shared links land on the same screen. The whole page's text and links stay available to screen readers in a hidden reading-order copy.
- Interaction is direct. Click or tap a choice to pick it. On a screen without choices (and on the map), a click or tap anywhere continues. Screens with choices ignore taps elsewhere. Scrolling, the mouse wheel, and swipes do nothing. There is no on-screen control strip. Each screen change is a browser-history step, so Back returns to the previous screen. On touch screens the prompts read "Tap the screen ..." instead of "Press SPACE BAR ...".
- The keyboard is an extra: SPACE, RETURN, or the right arrow continues (RETURN sizes up the situation on the travel screen); the left arrow goes back a screen; number keys choose (0 is Day 10); B or Esc returns to the menu. The up/down arrow keys do nothing. Every choice is also a tappable or clickable region, and hover or focus shows it in inverse video.
- Routes live at the site root: `/` (home, `?part=1` for Part I), `/<day-slug>/`, `/map/`, `/about/`. Old `/blog/...` links redirect (`vercel.json`, plus a client-side fallback). Production is https://route66-diary.vercel.app.
- Artwork is Apple II–native, generated with the Codex CLI `imagegen` skill (built-in `image_gen`). The prompts live in `scripts/apple2-art/prompts.mjs`; `jobs.mjs` and `run.sh` generate into `art-src/raw` (git-ignored), and `process.mjs` writes `public/assets/apple2/*.webp` plus `src/apple2/artManifest.json`. Pictures are drawn in the six hi-res colors so the load-time conversion (`src/apple2/dither.js`) keeps them crisp. Day landmark pictures are restyled from the original illustrations, which stay in `public/assets/part1|part2` as references. The Pontiac original is truncated, so `art-src/refs/day3-pontiac-top.png` is its complete upper part.
- The rental cars come from the Hertz receipts. Part I was a white 2026 Kia Sportage SUV (the `suv` sprite); Part II was a cream 2026 Hyundai Elantra sedan (the `sedan` sprite). Artwork and sprites must show the right car for each part.
- Handcrafted pixel sprites are used for game furniture: the two cars, roadside props, weather, maps, the splash logo, and the tombstone.
- Keep text per screen short. Story screens have a 280x112 picture, the animated drive, or a route map on top; a white caption bar with the trail stops; and at most six lines of diary text. Paragraphs are split across screens by `chunkText`, balanced and preferring sentence and clause breaks.
- Part I and Part II are separate chapters. Part II restarts at Day 1, and the home page defaults to Part II. Day lists run in day order, and the option number is the day number.
- Preserve all diary content verbatim: titles, dates, destinations, weather, summaries, every paragraph, and every trail stop. `src/storyPlan.js` assigns each stop to the paragraph that mentions it (each stop exactly once) and picks each paragraph's visual. On screen only, typographic characters map to the game's set (em dash to `--`, curly quotes to straight). The screen-reader layer keeps the original text.
- The diary text follows the earlier site at https://route66-trail-journal-david.davidyen1124.chatgpt.site (Part II Days 1–11, Chicago to Albuquerque), with the user's later corrections kept: we did not visit the Lincoln Tomb, so it stays out of Days 3 and 4 (text, stops, and pictures), and Day 3's summary says the day ended too late for Lincoln Home. Days 2 and 4 keep their original illustrations because the chatgpt.site's corrected Day 4 picture centres on the tomb.
- Each article runs: travel screen, landmark picture, a route-map summary, story screens, then the size-up menu (continue, go back a day, map, saved games, sound).
- Social previews use the exact title `Route 66 Trail Journal` and the two-journey description. Keep Open Graph and X metadata aligned.
